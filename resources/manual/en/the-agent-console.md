# The agent console

The console is the working side of Ticktz: every ticket that exists, sorted so that the ones that are yours are in front of you.

## Queues

A queue is a saved view of the ticket list — "unassigned hardware", "everything my team owns", "breaching today". Your desk sets them up; you pick one and work it.

**Queues** lists the ones available to you with a count on each, which is the fastest read on where the work is piling up. A queue can belong to a team, and then only that team sees it.

A queue also carries a default sort order, so opening one puts the ticket that should be looked at first at the top.

## The ticket list

**Tickets** is everything you are allowed to see, which is not necessarily everything. What you can see depends on your permissions and your teams: some agents see the whole desk, others see their own team's queues.

Narrow it with the filters along the top — status, priority, label, team, source, and whether it is yours or unassigned. The filters go into the address bar, so a filtered list is a link you can send to a colleague.

## Searching

The search box does two different things, and it works out which you meant:

- Type a **ticket key** like `SUP-1042` and you go straight to that ticket. You do not get a list of one result.
- Type **anything else** and it searches subjects.

If you know the key, the key is always the fastest route.

## Raising a ticket yourself

**New ticket** is for the work that did not arrive through the portal or by e-mail — somebody stopped you in the corridor, or you spotted something yourself.

The one field worth care is **requester**: it is who the ticket is *for*, not who typed it. Search for them by name or e-mail. Getting it right is what makes the ticket show up in their portal and their notifications.

Choosing a team narrows who the ticket can be assigned to, so pick the team first and the assignee second. If you pick an assignee and then change the team, the assignee is cleared — the new team may not include them.

## What the columns mean

The list shows status, priority, who it is for, who has it, and the service level clock. The clock is the one that repays learning to read; it is explained in *Service levels*.
