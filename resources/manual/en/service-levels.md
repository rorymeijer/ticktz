# Service levels

A service level is a promise about time: this kind of ticket gets a first reply within an hour and is resolved within eight. The clocks on a ticket are Ticktz keeping count.

## The clock runs in working hours

"Four hours" means four *working* hours. If your desk is open 08:30 to 17:30 on weekdays, a ticket that arrives at five o'clock on Friday with a four-hour target is not late on Saturday morning — it is due at half past eleven on Monday.

Public holidays and lunch breaks come out of it too, if your desk has set them. This is the single most common surprise about SLA clocks, and it is almost always the answer to "why does that say it has three hours left?".

## Reading the badge

The clock on the ticket list and in the panel is colour-coded:

- **Green** — met. The target was reached in time.
- **Amber** — running, and getting close.
- **Red** — past the target. Breached.
- **Blue** — paused.

A ticket can have more than one clock: typically one for a first response and one for a resolution. Meeting the first says nothing about the second.

## Pausing

Some statuses stop the clock — usually the ones that mean "waiting on the customer". Your desk decides which.

The time is paid back rather than forgiven: five working hours waiting on a customer push the deadline five working hours later. Nobody is penalised for the time a request spent on somebody else's desk, and nobody gets a free extension for putting a ticket on hold.

## When something breaches

A breach is recorded on the ticket and shown, and it stays shown. It is not erased by resolving the ticket afterwards — a target that was missed was missed, and a report that quietly lost the misses would not be worth running.

Your desk may have escalations attached: a breach can raise the priority, notify a team, or reassign. Those are configured, not automatic.

Every start, pause, resume and breach is recorded with its time. If there is ever an argument about whether a target was met, that history is what settles it.
