# Assets

The register of things your desk is asked about: laptops, servers, phones, licences.

It earns its keep in one moment — an agent opens a ticket about a laptop and can see whose it is, what it is connected to, and what else went wrong with it last month.

## Finding something

Search by asset tag, name, serial number or manufacturer. The asset tag is the sticker on the box, and it is the fastest route if you have the machine in front of you.

Filter by type and status to answer the other common question: what have we got, and how much of it is spare.

## What an asset page shows

- **Who has it**, and which organisation and team it belongs to.
- **Its status** — in stock, in use, in repair, retired. The status is what makes "how many spare laptops are there" answerable.
- **What it is connected to.** Assets can be linked to each other: a laptop to a docking station, a virtual machine to the host it runs on. When something breaks, these links are what tell you what else is about to.
- **The tickets it appears on**, which is the history somebody needs when the same machine comes back for the third time.
- **Whatever else your desk tracks about this type** — warranty dates, cost centre, licence counts. Each type of asset has its own fields.

## On a ticket

Attach the asset a ticket is about. It takes a moment and it is what makes the register worth having: without it, "this laptop keeps crashing" is a sentence, and with it, it is a machine with a history.

The ticket's requester is used to suggest their equipment first, so usually the right asset is already in the list.

## Keeping it true

A register nobody corrects becomes a register nobody trusts, and then nobody corrects it.

The two things worth keeping accurate above all others are **who holds it** and **its status**. Those are what the rest is read through.
