# Reports

Four questions about the desk, over a period you choose.

| Report | Answers |
| --- | --- |
| **Service level compliance** | Of the clocks that finished, how many were met |
| **Ticket volume** | How much arrived, and how much the desk cleared |
| **Agent workload** | Who resolved what, and how long they took |
| **How long it takes** | Time to a first reply, and time to a resolution |

## Reading them honestly

**Compliance counts clocks that finished.** A ticket still running is not counted as met or missed, because it is neither yet. A month that looks unusually good may simply have a lot of work still open.

**Volume shows arrived against cleared.** The two lines matter more than either number: a desk clearing as much as arrives is holding steady whatever the total, and one clearing less is accumulating a backlog whether or not it feels busy.

**Workload counts resolutions, not effort.** Somebody who closed forty password resets and somebody who spent the month on one migration are not comparable through this report, and reading it as a ranking is how it does damage.

## Narrowing it

Every report can be grouped by a dimension — team, queue, priority, request type, assignee — and filtered to a period. That is where most of the value is: "how long it takes" across the whole desk is a number, and "how long it takes, by priority" is a finding.

## Saving and exporting

A report you keep coming back to can be **saved** with its filters, and then it is one click rather than four fields.

**Export** gives you a CSV of what is on screen, for the numbers that have to end up in somebody else's spreadsheet.

## Why the numbers may lag

Reports read from a daily roll-up rather than counting tickets live. That is what keeps them quick on a desk with a million tickets, and it means today's figures may firm up after today. If a number looks wrong the morning after a busy day, check it again once the day has been rolled up.
