# Managing the knowledge base

## Categories

Articles live in categories, and the categories are what the portal shows. Keep them few and name them the way somebody with the problem would — "Printing", not "Output devices".

## Writing and publishing

An article is a **draft** until you publish it. Drafts are invisible to everybody but authors and editors, so nothing is lost by writing half of one.

**Public or internal** is the decision to take deliberately. Public articles appear in the portal for anybody who can raise a request; internal ones never leave the console. The safe default is internal, then publish outward once somebody has read it.

Moving an article from internal to public does not un-send it — check it before, not after.

## Keeping it worth reading

A knowledge base people trust is a small one where everything is current. A large one where a third is out of date gets abandoned, because the cost of checking is higher than the cost of asking.

Two habits do most of the work:

- **Write the article when you answer the question the second time.** Not the first — that might be a one-off.
- **Attach the article to the ticket it answered.** That is how you find out later which articles are earning their place, and which nobody has used in a year.

Write titles as the question somebody would type. "VPN disconnects every few minutes" gets found; "VPN troubleshooting (2024)" does not.
