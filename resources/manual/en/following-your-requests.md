# Following your requests

**My requests** lists everything you have asked for, newest first, with what is happening to each.

## Reading the status

Every request has a status. The names are your desk's own, but each one belongs to a category, and the category is what tells you where you stand:

- **New** — filed, nobody has picked it up yet.
- **Open** — somebody is working on it.
- **Pending** — waiting on something. Often that something is you: read the last message.
- **Resolved** — the desk believes it is done.
- **Closed** — finished and filed away.

If a request has been sitting at **Pending** for a while, the last message on it almost always explains why.

## Replying

Open a request and write at the bottom. Your reply goes to whoever is handling it and shows up in the conversation.

You can also reply to the e-mail Ticktz sent you, and it lands in the same place. Both work; use whichever is in front of you.

What you will not see is the desk's internal notes. Agents can write notes to each other on a ticket, and those stay on their side. Nothing you write is internal — everything you send is visible to the desk.

## When it is resolved

A resolved request that was not actually fixed does not need a new one. Reply to it and say so, and it comes back.

Your desk may close resolved requests automatically after a while. Once something is closed, raising a fresh request is the cleaner route.

## Your equipment

**Equipment** lists the things the desk has recorded as yours — laptops, phones, licences, whatever your organisation tracks. It is read-only: it is there so that when you raise a request you can say which machine, and so you can check what is registered to you.

If something on that list is not yours, or something of yours is missing, raise a request and say so. It matters more than it looks: that list is what the desk uses to work out who is affected when something breaks.
