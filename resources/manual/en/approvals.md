# Approvals

Some requests need somebody with authority to say yes before any work starts: a purchase, an access request, anything with a cost or a risk attached.

## Being asked

When you are an approver you get an e-mail and the request appears under **Approvals**. Open it, read what is being asked, and choose.

You can **approve** or **reject**, and you can say why. The reason is worth writing on a rejection — it is what the requester sees, and "no" without a reason produces a second, identical request a day later.

The e-mail also carries a link that lets you decide without signing in. That link is single-use and belongs to you; forwarding it hands somebody else your decision.

## What happens to the ticket

Until the approval is settled, the ticket cannot move past the point that required it. That is the whole mechanism: an approval is not a note asking somebody to remember, it is a gate.

When the last approver has decided, the ticket carries on — or stops, if the answer was no.

## Asking for one

From a ticket, **Approvals** in the right-hand column raises one.

Two ways to do it:

- **Run a configured workflow.** Your desk may have these set up for known cases, and they already know who has to be asked and in what order.
- **Ask named people now.** Pick who has to approve. If you pick more than one, say whether you need **any** of them or **all** of them.

The second exists because an agent who has to build an approval workflow before they can ask a manager a yes/no question will ask them in a chat window instead, and the answer will not be on the ticket.

You can pick anybody with an account, not only agents. An approver is very often a budget holder or a line manager who never opens the console.

## Several steps

A workflow with more than one step asks them in order: the second step does not open until the first is settled. That is how "your manager, then finance" is built.

Within a step, everybody is asked at the same time.

## Withdrawing

Whoever raised an approval can withdraw it — the request turned out not to be needed, or it was sent to the wrong person. An approver cannot withdraw the approval they were asked to give; they answer it instead.
